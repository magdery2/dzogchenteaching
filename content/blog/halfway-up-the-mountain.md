<p class="article-lead">For my precious friends on the contemplative path.</p>
<p>Even minor philosophical investigations, meditative and blissful experiences without proper mentorship can result in an egoistic belief that the practitioner of spirituality has awakened or reached a significant attainment.</p>
<p>Historically, psychological and spiritual self-deception as well as premature claims of awakening or insight end in a fall into despair or crisis; later these same mishaps can serve as a foundation for mature development and retrospectively can be seen as an important step in one's journey.</p>
<p>The continued experience of intrinsic awareness however, is a devastating defeat for the ego and when properly guided under the influence of compassion - results in transformative realization.</p>
<p>Every psychological advance individually and culturally is an extension of conscious-awareness, a coming to consciousness that can take place only through trial and error wisdom.</p>
<p>Therefore, an advance always begins with individuation, that is to say with the individual, conscious of their isolation, cutting a new path through hitherto untrodden territory. To do this he must first return to the fundamental fact of his own being, irrespective of all authority and tradition, and allow himself to become conscious of his distinctiveness - no mere unconscious follower of family, religion, society or other nominal convention.</p>
<p>While important for renunciation and finding path and purpose - individuation is not the apex of human development, and if spiritual development is stunted at this stage, then all too often the journeyman finds himself mired in existential or nihilistic crisis, lost in rebellion, addiction, or having accomplished little more than walking away from familial and societal norms.</p>
<p>Often this form of individuation manifests as an immature social provocateur, endless attempts at proving oneself, an angry condescending isolated culture warrior, or at worst, a Nietzschean descent into madness.</p>
<p>In modern culture too much importance is given to attaining a unique wholeness as a distinct individual, which often results in narcissistic self-cherishing, a loss of respect for convention, others, and the natural environment. Conversely, realization of the essential primacy of non-egoistic awareness always brings a loss of concern for individuation, and a greater empathetic sensitivity to the non-dual interconnectedness and inter-being of all things.</p>
<p>Surely, attaining psychological integration of the various forces that tear at our psyche is a precious process-oriented counseling goal, but it is not the ultimate goal of Awakening. Dissolution of the egoistic self as well as both divine and shadow archetypes into Dharmata - the natural ground that is radiant luminous awareness - comes with a price for individuated grasping and self-importance. Utter surrender and release of all that is represented as the failure of "I, me, my and mine" is necessary for the reality of realized awakening to be unveiled.</p>
<p>Carl Jung spoke of the failure vividly using Christian allegory,</p>
<blockquote class="article-blockquote">&ldquo;The utter failure came at the Crucifixion in the tragic words, &lsquo;My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?&rsquo; If you want to understand the full tragedy of those words you must realize what they meant: Christ saw that his whole life, devoted to the truth according to his best conviction, had been a terrible illusion. He had lived it to the full absolutely sincerely, he had made his honest experiment, but it was nevertheless a compensation. On the cross his mission deserted him. But because he had lived so fully and devotedly he won through to the Resurrection body.&rdquo;<cite> - Jung Speaks</cite></blockquote>
<p>As practitioners it is necessary to live in this tension between renunciate individuation and the ease of surrender into pure awareness as continuously as possible - walking between the form of individuation and formless awareness.</p>
<p>Continuous application of this process leads to an increasingly refined and living awareness in which the need for individuation gradually dissolves, the tension spoken of above disappears and all activity becomes a natural reflection of pure awareness itself.</p>
<p class="article-signoff"> - Michael Gregory</p>
