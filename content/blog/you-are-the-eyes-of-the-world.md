<p>As contemplatives and meditators we are to relax our clinging to the illusion of everyday life and allow the wider perspective of total openness to flood through us and light the world from within.</p>
<p>When we are able to relax like this, the energy invested maintaining a typical life of neurotic busyness is freed and we can release into our perfect natural state and condition.</p>
<p>In the process of letting go of our illusions, we can let go of one thing, only to replace it with another. We usually think of experience in dualistic terms: good or bad, enjoyable or painful, dull or enriching, desirable or awful. Even in the spiritual context experiences are evaluated according to how beneficial, transcendent, calming, insightful, or powerful they may be.</p>
<p>Direct yogic perception points us to the experience that is unqualified yet includes and is at the heart of all possible experiences. Many names can be given to this experience. Yet, as language always partakes of dualism, the various names could never be more than partial reflections of something beautiful and beyond words.</p>
<p>Our pilgrimage will concern itself with the ultimate inner reality of all life - awareness itself. We will present the practices of one of the most holistic teachings of Buddhism - Dzogchen - and an all-encompassing vision of reality including the techniques for experiencing this vision, how to sustain this experience in daily life, and make the ongoing experience so vivid and ever present that no fear or doubt could ever overcome it.</p>
<p>The actuality of experiencing pure awareness is ephemeral and unlimited and is always available and energetic. The various moment-to-moment and day-to-day experiences that we all perceive are simply the play, the dance of this energy - the awareness of true awakening.</p>
