There are teachings we may encounter in a single evening. There are others that benefit from being approached slowly, with time to study, contemplate and practice.

The Dra Thal Gyur, a root tantra of the Seventeen Tantras of the Dzogchen Upadeśa tradition, belongs to the second kind. This first course begins a longer study of the text alongside Vimalamitra’s commentary.

A foundational Dzogchen text

Within the Dzogchen Upadeśa tradition, the Dra Thal Gyur is the root tantra of the Seventeen Tantras. Course 32 begins a careful study of this foundational text through translation, commentary, meditation and practice.

Why this text?

In the introductory teachings, Lama Michael returns to a practical question that can remain alive even after years of study: what is actually being pointed to in the nature-of-mind instructions? The Dra Thal Gyur is compelling here because its language is meant to be investigated in meditation and lived experience, not simply collected as philosophy.

What actually is being pointed to?▶

Rather than presenting a collection of disconnected ideas, the course begins a planned six-course, 72-week journey in which the language, view and questions of the text can be revisited.

The invitation is not simply to collect Dzogchen concepts, but to remain with the teachings long enough to investigate what they point toward.
Each class brings together:

Translation and study of the root text · traditional commentary · Lama Michael’s explanation · guided meditation · contemplative practice between classes

Why begin now?

These first twelve weeks establish the terms, questions and contemplative framework that later study will build upon. Registration also includes the introductory class, so students can begin with the foundations already introduced.

Who is this for?

This serious study and practice course may especially appeal to practitioners seeking continuity with a traditional text. Prior familiarity with the Dra Thal Gyur or previous Dzogchen study is not required: the introductory class is included with registration so students can begin with the foundational principles Lama Michael has introduced.

Begin with the introductory class. When you register, you will have access to the introductory class, the first class Lama Michael taught in this introductory series, now included in the course curriculum.

What does “self-arisen awareness” mean in practice?

A short introduction before the course begins

One question at the center of Lama Michael’s introductory teachings is expressed as Rangjung Rigpa: self-arisen, unfabricated awareness.

If awareness is self-arisen, it is not something meditation manufactures. That changes the question from “How do I produce a better state?” to a quieter inquiry:

Can awareness be recognised while thought is present? What happens when an appearance is examined within the awareness in which it appears?

These questions lead into further teachings explored in the opening classes: self-appearance and great purity, and everything as self-arisen primordial wisdom. The phrases are concise; what they point toward asks for careful study.

Can sound be separated from hearing?▶

Course 32 combines translation and textual study with guided meditation and contemplative practice. The intention is not only to recognise terms, but to investigate how the teachings may be met within ordinary experience.

Is Course 32 right for you?

What to expect from the first 12 weeks

As the course approaches, an important question is how much previous Dzogchen study is needed to participate.

Course 32 is a serious study and practice course, but you do not need prior Dzogchen study or familiarity with the Dra Thal Gyur itself. The introductory class, included with registration, offers the foundation Lama Michael has begun to establish.

Who this is for

This course may especially appeal to practitioners who are looking for more than occasional exposure to Dzogchen teachings and who value the continuity of working with a traditional text over time. The course begins at the first twelve weeks of a planned six-course study.

Why the King of Tantras must be approached through practice.▶
The course may suit you if you are looking for:

A sustained relationship with one traditional text · a regular rhythm of study and meditation · clarification of terms and context · room to investigate rather than rush toward conclusions

Each week includes:

Textual study: the Dra Thal Gyur and Vimalamitra’s commentary.

Translation & commentary: terminology, context and the Dzogchen view expressed in the text.

Guided meditation & integration: contemplative investigation in the circumstances of ordinary life.

Recordings are available through Teachable for students who cannot attend every class live.

This is not intended as a general survey of Dzogchen. It is the beginning of a careful, twelve-week study of a foundational tantra of the Upadeśa tradition. The value of beginning now is that the first course lays the language and contemplative orientation for the sequence that follows.
